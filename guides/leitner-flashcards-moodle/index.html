How learners use the Leitner box in Moodle

LeitBox sorts the cards of an activity into six stacks: New, Beginner, Learner, Advanced, Experienced and Expert. Learners choose which stack to practise. After each card they rate themselves, and the rating decides which stack the card goes to. There is no fixed schedule; learners practise at their own pace.

Updated 6 November 2026

What you need

  • Moodle 4.1 to 5.1 with LeitBox installed by an administrator (download from GitHub)
  • A LeitBox activity with cards in the course
  • A computer, tablet or phone; LeitBox runs in the browser

Step by step

Step 1 of 6
  1. Step 1 of 6

    Choose a stack

    The overview shows the six stacks with the number of cards in each. A click on a stack starts a round with its cards; here Learner with three cards.

  2. Step 2 of 6

    Think first, use the hint if needed

    The card shows the question. If the teacher has added a hint, Show hint reveals it.

  3. Step 3 of 6

    Turn the card

    Tap to flip turns the card and shows the answer.

  4. Step 4 of 6

    Rate yourself

    Got it moves the card one stack on. Again means you were not sure: the card stays in its stack.

  5. Step 5 of 6

    Hard: one stack back

    Hard moves the card back one stack, but never below Beginner. The rest of the learning progress stays as it is.

  6. Step 6 of 6

    Round summary

    At the end of the round, LeitBox shows how many cards were rated Got it, Again and Hard. Back to dashboard returns to the stacks.

Good to know

Learners decide

LeitBox does not set dates or send reminders. Learners choose which stack to practise and when.

Progress stays in Moodle

The learning progress is stored in your Moodle and is part of the course backup when user data is included.

Method

The Leitner box goes back to the science journalist Sebastian Leitner (1972). LeitBox follows his idea of moving cards between stacks.
